Waterfall Model (Best when requirements are fixed) Choose Waterfall if:
Requirements are well-defined and unlikely to change (e.g., compliance-heavy payroll systems).
The client prefers detailed documentation and a linear sequence of phases (requirements → design → implementation → testing → deployment).  There is limited stakeholder involvement after initial planning.  The organization emphasizes predictability and control over adaptability.

Agile Methodologies (Best when adaptability is needed) Choose Agile if:
Requirements are evolving or not fully known upfront.  The client expects regular feedback and iterative improvements.  The team is cross-functional and collaborative.  The project benefits from early and continuous delivery of working software.

19. (10 points). Grasp Patterns
Controller Pattern
   * PaymentController handles system operations (makePayPalPayment(), makeGiftCardPayment()).
   * It mediates between the Customer and the payment classes, centralizing control logic.

Creator Pattern
   * PaymentController creates PayPalPayment and GiftCardPayment objects (createPayment() → create()).
   * This follows GRASP Creator: the class that contains or uses another object is responsible for its creation.

Expert Pattern:
   * Payment (and it's children) have the necessary information to process_payment().

Polymorphism Pattern
   * Both PayPalPayment and GiftCardPayment implement a common interface (likely subclass of Payment) with a process() method.
   * PaymentController can call processPayment() without knowing the specific payment type — achieving dynamic dispatch.

Nice to have but less important than the other 4.
Low Coupling / High Cohesion
   * PaymentController interacts only with the Payment interface, not specific implementations.
   * Keeps classes focused on single responsibilities (each payment type processes its own logic).
